How this is calculated

Each row is one guess at the year’s medical bills, measured as the total the providers bill before insurance. The same bills are run through both plans.

  1. Taxable income is household income, minus your deduction, minus that plan’s annual premium. Premiums are treated as pre-tax. The federal marginal rate is the 2026 bracket that taxable income falls in. If taxable income is zero or negative, that rate is 0%.
  2. The after-tax premium is the annual premium times one minus your federal rate and any state rate you enter. When the payroll switch is on, that price also drops by 7.65% for FICA.
  3. Your share of a bill is everything up to the deductible, plus coinsurance on the rest. It stops at the plan’s out-of-pocket maximum.
  4. HRA total cost is the after-tax premium plus whatever part of your share the HRA deposit does not cover. Leftover HRA dollars are not yours, so they are not subtracted.
  5. HSA tax savings equal your own contribution times the federal rate, plus state, plus 7.65% when the payroll switch is on. The employer’s HSA deposit is already tax-free, so it is not counted again.
  6. HSA total cost is the after-tax premium, plus your share, minus the employer deposit, minus those tax savings. The contribution itself is not a cost — it stays in the account. A negative total means you come out ahead.
  7. HSA left for next year is the employer deposit plus your contribution, minus your share. It cannot go below zero.

Assumptions

  • Coinsurance starts at 20%. You pay billed charges in full until the deductible, then that percentage, and never more than the out-of-pocket maximum. Change the percent if your plan differs.
  • The HRA pays any out-of-pocket cost up to its balance. Money it does not spend is the employer’s, not yours.
  • Only federal income tax is included unless you set a state rate. The default state rate is 0%.
  • The HSA starts the year empty. Employer and employee contributions are added, then care is paid from the account. A leftover balance is yours next year.
  • One marginal rate is applied to the whole premium and the whole HSA contribution. A contribution that would cross into a lower bracket is still valued at the higher rate. The bracket is measured after the premium and before the HSA contribution.
  • FICA savings (7.65%) apply to both premiums and to your HSA contribution only when the payroll switch is on.
  • Your employee contribution cannot exceed the HSA limit minus the employer deposit. The 2026 limits in the form are $8,750 for family coverage and $4,400 for self-only coverage.
  • This is a simplified comparison, not tax, legal, or benefits advice. Check your plan documents and a tax professional before you enroll.

2026 federal brackets and the standard deduction are from IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32. HSA contribution limits are from IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-19. This page does not file anything and is not tax advice.
